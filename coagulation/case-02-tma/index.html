Language matters

Historically, “TTP” was sometimes used almost synonymously with “TMA.” Contemporary practice reserves TTP for severe ADAMTS13 deficiency, because the label implies a specific emergency treatment pathway.

03 · Background

Start with the syndrome

TMA is a clinicopathologic pattern of endothelial injury, platelet-rich microthrombi, red-cell fragmentation, and ischemic organ damage.

Four signals to integrate

01

Mechanical red-cell injury

Red cells fragment while traversing narrowed, thrombus-lined microvessels. The resulting intravascular hemolysis raises LDH and consumes circulating haptoglobin.

Source caseSchistocytes, LDH 535 IU/L, and haptoglobin below 15 mg/dL establish microangiopathic hemolysis in context.

Do not delay emergency care

When immune TTP is clinically likely, draw pretreatment ADAMTS13 testing and begin appropriate urgent therapy while the result is pending. Reassess the diagnosis when the result returns.

Pattern → mechanism

A disciplined TMA evaluation

Reveal each layer of the workup and what it contributes.

01Confirm microangiopathic hemolysis

CBC and smear, reticulocyte count, LDH, bilirubin, haptoglobin, and direct antiglobulin testing. Schistocytes support fragmentation but are not specific for TTP.

02Assess coagulation and common mimics

PT/INR, aPTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer, medication review, blood pressure, infection evaluation, and clinical assessment help distinguish DIC and other causes.

03Draw ADAMTS13 before plasma therapy

Activity testing is central when TTP is possible. If activity is severely deficient, inhibitor or antibody evaluation helps classify immune versus congenital disease.

04Characterize organ injury and triggers

Renal studies, neurologic assessment, transplant status, graft function, drug exposure, and selected complement evaluation help define the mechanism.

05Use biopsy selectively

Microthrombi and cortical necrosis support renal TMA, but morphology alone usually cannot distinguish TTP from drug-, transplant-, or complement-associated TMA.

Why terminology matters

The label changes the pathway

Clinical nuance

Changing tacrolimus to another calcineurin inhibitor does not automatically remove the endothelial-injury risk. Management requires coordinated assessment of the implicated exposure, graft needs, concurrent triggers, and evolving organ injury.

08 · Summary

The diagnostic playbook

Carry these distinctions into the next thrombocytopenic patient with hemolysis and organ injury.

01

Recognize the syndrome

MAHA, thrombocytopenia, and organ injury should trigger urgent evaluation for TMA.

02

Do not equate TMA with TTP

Schistocytes and elevated LDH establish fragmentation—not severe ADAMTS13 deficiency.

03

Estimate, then test

PLASMIC estimates the probability of severe deficiency; ADAMTS13 testing addresses the mechanism.

04

Honor the 10% threshold

A mildly reduced activity such as 62% is not diagnostic of TTP.

05

Integrate the context

Transplantation, calcineurin-inhibitor exposure, kidney-predominant injury, and biopsy findings support secondary TMA.

06

Treat the mechanism

Urgent TTP therapy differs from management of drug-, transplant-, and complement-associated TMA.

Case resolution

Tacrolimus-/transplant-associated TMA—not confirmed TTP

The patient had a genuine TMA syndrome with schistocytes, hemolysis, thrombocytopenia, renal cortical necrosis, and microthrombi. However, ADAMTS13 activity was 62%, far above the severe-deficiency threshold. The most defensible contemporary classification is tacrolimus-associated or transplant-associated TMA.
