Section 3 of 8

Background

TTP is a thrombotic microangiopathy driven by severe functional deficiency of ADAMTS13, the protease that controls ultra-large von Willebrand factor multimers.

Severe ADAMTS13 deficiency
Typically <10% activity
→
Ultra-large VWF persists
Platelet-adhesive strings remain
→
Platelet-rich microthrombi
Ischemia, thrombocytopenia, MAHA

The bedside pattern

Microangiopathic hemolysis

  • Schistocytes
  • Elevated LDH and indirect bilirubin
  • Low haptoglobin
  • Reticulocytosis

Organ involvement

  • Neurologic symptoms are common
  • Cardiac and gastrointestinal ischemia may occur
  • Renal injury is often less prominent than in some other TMAs
  • The historic “pentad” is neither required nor expected

Immune or congenital?

Immune-mediated TTP

Acquired autoantibodies

Antibodies may neutralize ADAMTS13, accelerate its clearance, or do both. Functional inhibitor and antibody tests therefore provide related—but nonidentical—information.

Congenital TTP

Biallelic ADAMTS13 variants

Congenital TTP is established by compatible clinical and laboratory findings plus molecular confirmation. A single negative inhibitor test is not sufficient proof.

Time matters.
When clinical suspicion is high, collect plasma for ADAMTS13 studies before plasma-based therapy if feasible, but do not allow testing turnaround to create a dangerous treatment delay.

Section 4 of 8

Core Educational Content

“ADAMTS13 activity low, inhibitor negative” is a result pattern—not a final diagnosis.

The central rule
A negative functional inhibitor assay does not exclude immune-mediated TTP.

Ask what each assay detects

Activity

ADAMTS13 activity assay

Estimates functional cleavage of a VWF-based substrate. Severe deficiency strongly supports TTP in the appropriate phenotype.

Function

Inhibitor assay

Tests whether patient plasma reduces ADAMTS13 activity in vitro. Low-titer or non-neutralizing antibodies may escape detection.

Binding

Antibody immunoassay

Detects binding anti-ADAMTS13 antibodies, including antibodies that promote clearance without measurable in vitro inhibition.

A discordant-result checklist

  1. Reassess the phenotype. Is this thrombocytopenia plus microangiopathic hemolysis with a plausible TTP presentation?
  2. Check timing. Was the sample obtained before plasma exchange, plasma infusion, or other therapy that could alter activity or antibody concentration?
  3. Inspect specimen quality. Consider in vitro hemolysis, icterus, lipemia, wrong anticoagulant, and EDTA contamination.
  4. Know the method. Different assay formats do not share identical interference profiles.
  5. Use complementary testing. Repeat activity/inhibitor testing and consider anti-ADAMTS13 IgG when results remain discordant.
  6. Reserve genetics for the right question. Congenital TTP requires a compatible longitudinal phenotype and molecular confirmation.
46%

In the retrospective series highlighted by the source document, many severely deficient samples initially lacked a detectable inhibitor. The broader lesson is method dependence.

Analytical vs clinical sensitivity

Analytical sensitivity asks how little analyte a method can detect. Clinical sensitivity asks how often the test is positive in patients with the disease.

Section 5 of 8

Mechanisms

Two antibody behaviors can converge on the same biological endpoint: severe ADAMTS13 deficiency.

Neutralizing

Inhibitor antibody

Binds functionally important sites and blocks protease activity. It produces inhibition in vitro and is usually detected by a functional mixing assay.

  • Activity reduced in vivo
  • Inhibitor assay often positive
  • Binding antibody assay may also be positive
Non-neutralizing

Clearance antibody

Binds ADAMTS13 without measurably blocking the assay substrate reaction, but promotes accelerated removal of the enzyme in vivo.

  • Antigen and activity fall in vivo
  • Functional inhibitor assay may be negative
  • Binding antibody assay may be positive
Reality is not binary.
A patient may have a mixture of antibody populations, and antibody concentration or behavior can change over time.

Case 2

The Hemolyzed Sample

After traumatic venipuncture, a grossly hemolyzed specimen yields ADAMTS13 activity of 12%. The clinical picture is inconsistent with TTP.

What should the laboratory recommend?

Correct. The repeat, non-hemolyzed specimen in this case measured 72% activity.
Not yet. Troubleshoot a visibly compromised specimen before applying a disease label.
Mechanism
Plasma hemoglobin can alter VWF cleavage, and sample color can interfere with fluorescence-based detection.
Case 3

EDTA Contamination

An ADAMTS13 specimen is contaminated with EDTA. Activity returns below 10%.

Why can this result be falsely low?

Correct. Repeat testing on correctly collected citrate platelet-poor plasma measured 88% activity.
Think preanalytically. EDTA affects enzyme function in the tube.

Section 8 of 8

Summary

The absence of evidence is not evidence of absence—especially when the assay was never designed to detect every pathogenic mechanism.

  1. Start with pretest probability. Thrombocytopenia plus microangiopathic hemolysis and relatively preserved renal function should place TTP high on the differential.
  2. Severe ADAMTS13 deficiency matters. Activity below 10% strongly supports TTP in the appropriate clinical setting.
  3. Negative inhibitor ≠ nonimmune. Low-titer neutralizing antibodies and non-neutralizing clearance antibodies may not register in a functional inhibitor assay.
  4. Use complementary methods. Functional inhibitor assays and anti-ADAMTS13 immunoassays answer different questions.
  5. Interrogate discordance. Revisit timing, therapy, sample quality, anticoagulant, and method-specific interference.
  6. Hemolysis can mislead. When in vitro hemolysis is suspected, obtain a fresh specimen if possible.
  7. EDTA can artifactually suppress activity. Properly collected platelet-poor citrate plasma is normally preferred.
  8. A laboratory test estimates biology. Method literacy and clinical context are core competencies.
Resident sign-out language
“Severe ADAMTS13 deficiency is present. A negative functional inhibitor assay does not exclude immune-mediated TTP; consider anti-ADAMTS13 IgG testing and/or repeat inhibitor testing in clinical context. Correlate with specimen integrity and treatment timing.”
