Clinical frame

TTP is a treatment emergency. Laboratory testing refines diagnosis, classification, and prognosis; treatment decisions should not wait for a send-out result when clinical suspicion is high.

03 · Background

Start with the syndrome

TTP lives within the thrombotic microangiopathy differential. The first laboratory task is to recognize MAHA and separate the pattern from common mimics.

The diagnostic constellation

01

Mechanical hemolysis

Red cells traversing platelet-rich microvascular thrombi fragment, producing schistocytes and intravascular hemolysis. A negative direct antiglobulin test supports a nonimmune mechanism.

Etiologic classification

Finding the antibody

Activity confirms functional deficiency. Antibody testing helps distinguish acquired immune TTP from hereditary TTP.

More specific

Bethesda inhibitor assay

Patient plasma is mixed with normal pooled plasma. Lower-than-expected recovery of activity indicates a neutralizing inhibitor.

  • Positive in roughly two-thirds of TTP cases

08 · Summary

The laboratory playbook

Carry these decisions into your next thrombotic microangiopathy workup.

01

Recognize the signal

MAHA plus marked thrombocytopenia should trigger urgent evaluation for TTP.

02

Estimate pretest probability

Use PLASMIC to estimate the likelihood of severe ADAMTS13 deficiency—not as a replacement for testing or judgment.

03

Collect before treatment

Use citrated platelet-poor plasma. Avoid EDTA, serum, preventable hemolysis, delayed processing, and repeated freeze–thaw cycles.

04

Measure function first

An ADAMTS13 activity below 10% is strongly supportive of TTP in the appropriate clinical setting.

05

Characterize the cause

Use Bethesda and antibody ELISA methods reflexively to identify neutralizing and non-neutralizing autoantibodies.

06

Keep monitoring

Persistent low activity or inhibitor during remission signals meaningful relapse risk and supports close follow-up.

Case resolution

Severe deficiency confirmed immune TTP

The patient’s acute specimen showed ADAMTS13 activity <5% with a 1.8-BU inhibitor. Neurologic symptoms resolved after the first plasma exchange; thrombocytopenia and hemolysis resolved after additional exchanges and prednisone. During remission, activity remained low at 15% with a 0.6-BU inhibitor, supporting close relapse surveillance.
